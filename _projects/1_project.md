Our research focuses on understanding why AI systems make particular decisions and how these decisions can be communicated effectively to users. We develop methods for interpreting retrieval models, foundation models, and large language models, with the goal of improving transparency, trust, and accountability.

Representative Publications
A Counterfactual Explanation Framework for Retrieval Models (ACL 2026)

We propose a counterfactual explanation framework that identifies the minimal changes required to alter retrieval outcomes. The framework helps users understand which features and document characteristics most influence ranking decisions.

Dissecting Bias in LLMs: A Mechanistic Interpretability Perspective (TMLR 2025)

This work investigates how biases emerge within the internal mechanisms of large language models. By combining mechanistic interpretability techniques with bias analysis, we identify pathways that contribute to undesirable model behaviour.

Unraveling the Influence of Training Data and Internal Structures in Large Language Models for Enhanced Explainability (AAAI 2025)

This study examines how training data and model architecture influence explanations generated by LLMs, providing insights into the relationship between model internals and observed behaviour.

The Curious Case of IR Explainability: Explaining Document Scores within and across Ranking Models (SIGIR 2020)
